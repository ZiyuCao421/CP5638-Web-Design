Project Plan — Vcare Opticals Website

Student: ZiyuCao  ·  Unit: CP5638 ICT  ·  Assessment: A2 Project Plan & Small Website (Individual)

Goal

Provide clear Vcare Opticals information and a convenient online appointment-request path for new and existing patients.

Vcare Opticals has served the community for over 25 years and has expanded its services with advanced eye-care technology, online appointment booking and an additional paediatric optometrist. The website will help patients understand these services, meet the team, read eye-health guidance, check clinic details and request an appointment.

Numeric targets are defined in the Success Evaluation so the goal remains focused on the outcome.

Success Evaluation

Success is measured after launch with booking, conversion and engagement data. Technical and usability checks support these business measures but do not replace them.

Evaluation plan

If booking requests or conversion are below target after three months, review the online booking path, appointment options and form fields with reception before adding more content.

Target Audience

Local adults and families seeking routine eye examinations, prescription updates, optical products, or reliable eye-health information.

Within that group, two segments matter most: parents of children who need vision care, given the practice’s paediatric and family optometry offering and professional development in myopia control and vision therapy; and adults over 40 with a chronic health condition such as diabetes or hypertension, who need diagnostic screening and coordinated care alongside their general practitioner. A secondary audience is people who already wear glasses or contact lenses and need a prescription update or a new set of frames. The site is deliberately not aimed at people seeking cosmetic procedures or a retail-only frame shop. LASIK information is limited to the consultation, assessment and care support described by the client; the site does not claim to perform laser surgery.

The design serves this audience in the following ways:

  • Online booking is the primary action. Calls-to-action take visitors to an accessible appointment-request form, while the phone number and address remain easy to find for patients who prefer personal assistance.
  • Legible, generous typography. Body text is a minimum of 17px with generous line height and strong colour contrast, and the layout collapses to a single column on a phone. This is deliberate: the audience includes older adults and people whose vision is the reason for their visit, so readability is part of the clinical message rather than a decorative choice.
  • Scannable content. The client’s long email content is rewritten into short sections, checklists, a trading-hours table and clearly labelled headings, so a patient can find the one fact they need in seconds instead of reading a paragraph.
  • Reassurance through the team section. Qualifications, years of experience and professional focus are shown for each staff member, because parents choosing a paediatric optometrist and adults with chronic conditions both need to know who will be looking after them.
  • Education as a trust builder. The Eye Health page explains why annual eye examinations matter in plain language, answering the questions patients are often reluctant to ask in the chair.
  • Mobile-first and light to load. Most enquiries arrive on a phone, so every call to action, phone number and address is reachable with one thumb on a small screen, and each photograph is resized and compressed to the size it is displayed at.
  • No dead ends. The footer repeats the address, phone number, fax, email address and trading hours on every page, and every page links to every other page.

Images

Only supplied assignment images are used, including the general optometry photographs and the four supplied staff photographs. No image has been generated, downloaded from the internet or linked from an external address. Each original is between 2 MB and 6 MB and up to 10,752 px wide, so every image is cropped to the aspect ratio it is displayed at, resized to that display size and saved as a progressive JPEG (the logo is kept as PNG). This reduces the image weight of the site from roughly 30 MB to about 1 MB, which matters for patients on a mobile connection. Every image carries descriptive alternative text, and photographs of equipment are captioned or noted as illustrative so they are never presented as this clinic’s own devices.

Design and technical approach

  • Hand-built HTML5 and CSS. No templates, frameworks or CSS libraries such as Bootstrap are used, in line with the assignment requirements. One external stylesheet (css/styles.css) is linked from every page.
  • No external resources of any kind. The site loads no web fonts, no external images, no embedded maps and no third-party scripts, and it contains no links to external websites, because the client supplied none. Every page therefore remains complete and readable when the project is unpacked from the ZIP with no network connection. Typography uses system fonts only.
  • Consistent visual system. A teal, mint and amber palette with a serif display face and a sans-serif body face, repeated identically across all five pages: the same header, navigation, footer, button styles and spacing rhythm.
  • Accessibility. Semantic landmarks (header, nav, main, section, footer), a skip link, one h1 per page with a correct heading order, descriptive alternative text, visible keyboard focus styling, colour contrast of at least WCAG AA, and aria-current="page" on the active navigation item.
  • Images are never presented as portraits. The supplied set contains no photographs of the practice’s staff, so the team page uses text profiles and a captioned equipment image; presenting a stock photograph as a real practitioner would be misleading.
  • Separation from the plan. The project plan is a separate document and is deliberately not linked from any page of the website.

Build sequence, validation and risks

  1. Confirm the goal, success measures and target audience with the client (this document).
  2. Agree the information architecture and draw the site flowchart (Figure 1).
  3. Prepare the supplied images: crop, resize and compress to the sizes actually displayed.
  4. Build the shared structure — stylesheet, header, navigation, footer, buttons.
  5. Build the five pages in navigation order, moving the client content into each page.
  6. Run the evaluation plan above: link check, validation, and desktop and narrow-screen review.
  7. Release, then record monthly enquiry counts and review at one, three and six months.

Main risks and mitigations: no photograph was supplied for Dr. Daniel Wilson, so the team page uses an explicitly labelled initials placeholder rather than a misleading portrait. The online form is an appointment request until it is connected to a secure production booking service. If the site generates requests but few confirmed appointments, the booking path and available appointment options should be reviewed with reception.
